An apron. A hangar. Two inspection drones. An aircraft taxiing. One drone slips behind the hangar. What should the screen say now?

For every tracked object, Mowvee gives one of three answers. I see it. I don't see it: here is where it can be. Or: I don't know. What it has not observed, it marks unverified, never empty. A possible-presence warning stays on until a sensor looks again.

Radar, LiDAR, visible and thermal cameras, and what aircraft and drones broadcast. All simulated from public data sheets. Each sensor has blind spots, and Mowvee draws them.

Fog, then heavy rain. By calculation, the cameras' reach collapses, and Mowvee declares it, per sensor. Only the radar is left: no redundancy. If the radar also fails, a fault we inject, reliability is lost: warnings would come too late.

Two drones fly close. Too close to separate: unresolved tracks. Then they touch. Event: contact, inferred. A possible-debris volume opens, and never shrinks on its own. Our weak point: in simulation, the contact was declared within two seconds in only one run out of sixteen.

An engine fails while the aircraft taxis, with a flash. The flash blinds the cameras: Mowvee marks them unreliable, in thirty-two runs out of thirty-two. In the model, the radar alone still sees the aircraft. Behind the smoke: not observed.

First, the limits. Everything you saw is simulated from public data sheets. Almost half of the data-sheet values are our assumptions. No physical sensor. Object classes are labelled by hand. The rules for sudden events are not yet in the prototype. For aircraft in flight, the warning does not yet come in time. Total delay is not measured. What holds, in simulation: a drone hidden behind a building was announced in time in two hundred passes out of two hundred. The warning goes off only once the whole zone has been looked at: zero passes out of six hundred lost it.

The core comes from our short-range version: a person behind an obstacle, a depth camera, the same three answers.

Mowvee. Seen, not seen, unknown: declared for every track. It observes and warns. No output is a command. By S D M S.
