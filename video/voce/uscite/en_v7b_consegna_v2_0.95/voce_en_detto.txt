A guarded aerospace site. A drone heads in, confirmed about nine hundred and fifty metres out: in time for the operator. What should the screen say when no sensor sees it?

MoVi gives every track one of three answers. I see it. I don't see it: here is where it can be. Or: I don't know. In simulation, it says empty only where it looked, if the site map is right. A possible-presence warning stays on until the whole zone is checked, or the object is seen again.

Radar, LiDAR, cameras, aircraft and drone broadcasts: all simulated from public data sheets. Where a sensor cannot see a drone, the zone stays unverified.

Dense fog. MoVi declares each sensor's reach. For a small drone, radar range drops from one thousand and sixty-two to one thousand and forty-one metres: a small change, not an independent check.

Two drones collide. MoVi infers the event two point two seconds later; within two seconds in fifty-one runs of sixty. A debris zone opens. It grows, does not shrink by looking, and cannot tell who is at risk.

An engine fails on take-off, with a flash. MoVi declares the blinded cameras unreliable, within three cycles in every simulated run. Only the radar is left: here it loses the aircraft at times, and MoVi says so. The debris warning does not discriminate: about ninety-six tracks per run.

First, the limits. No physical sensor: all simulated. Almost half the values are assumed. Classes are labelled by hand. For aircraft in flight, warnings come too late. A drone that stops can slip out of the warning: twelve runs of two hundred. What holds, in simulation: a drone on the site was announced in time in all two hundred runs, its position never declared empty, with the site map declared complete.
