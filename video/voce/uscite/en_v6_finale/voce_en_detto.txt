A guarded aerospace site. A drone heads for it, confirmed about nine hundred and fifty metres out: in time for the operator. What should the screen say when no sensor sees it?

MoVi gives every track one of three answers. I see it. I don't see it: here is where it can be. Or: I don't know. It says empty only where it has looked. A possible-presence warning stays on until the whole zone is looked at, or the object is seen again.

Radar, LiDAR, cameras, and what aircraft and drones broadcast: all simulated from public data sheets. Where a sensor cannot see a drone, the zone stays unverified.

Dense fog. MoVi declares each sensor's reach. For a small drone, the radar's range drops from one thousand and sixty-two to one thousand and forty-one metres: a small change, and not an independent check.

Two drones collide. MoVi infers contact two point two seconds later; within two seconds in fifty-one runs of sixty. A debris zone opens. It grows, does not shrink by looking, and cannot tell who is at risk.

An engine fails on the take-off run, with a flash. MoVi declares the blinded cameras unreliable, within three cycles in every simulated run. Only the radar is left: in this run it loses the aircraft for seconds at a time, and MoVi says so. The debris warning does not discriminate: about ninety-six tracks.

First, the limits. All simulated from public data sheets: no physical sensor. Almost half the values are assumed. Classes are labelled by hand. For aircraft in flight, warnings come too late. A drone that stops can slip out of the warning: twelve runs of two hundred. What holds, in simulation: a drone on the site was announced in time in two hundred runs of two hundred, and its position never declared empty, with the site map declared complete.

The core comes from our short-range version: a person behind an obstacle, a depth camera, the same three answers.

MoVi. Seen, not seen, unknown: declared for every track. It observes and warns. No output is a command. By SDMs.
