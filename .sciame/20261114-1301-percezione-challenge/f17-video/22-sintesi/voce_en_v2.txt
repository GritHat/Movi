An apron. A hangar. Two inspection drones. An aircraft taxiing. One drone slips behind the hangar. What should the screen say now?

MoVi declares what it has not observed. For every tracked object, one of three answers. I see it. I don't see it: here is where it can be. Or: I don't know. What it has not observed, it marks unverified, never empty. A possible-presence warning stays on until a sensor looks again.

Radar, LiDAR, visible and thermal cameras, and what aircraft and drones broadcast. All simulated from public data sheets. Each sensor has blind spots, and MoVi draws them.

Fog, then heavy rain. By calculation, the cameras' reach collapses, and MoVi declares it, per sensor. Only the radar is left: no redundancy. If the radar also fails, a fault we inject, reliability is lost: warnings would come too late.

Two drones fly close. Too close to separate: unresolved tracks. Then they touch. Event: contact, inferred. A possible-debris volume opens, and never shrinks on its own. The operator is warned: a person is underneath. The ground stays unverified until inspected.

An engine fails on the take-off run, with a flash. The flash blinds the cameras: MoVi marks them unreliable. In the model, the radar alone still sees the aircraft. Behind the smoke: not observed. Downstream: possible debris, unverified.
